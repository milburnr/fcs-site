When you search for commercial general contractors near you in Clearwater, the field isn't as even as it looks — and the difference between a properly licensed, always-prime contractor and a broker who hands your project to subs can decide whether your building passes inspection on the first try. Here's what to check before you sign anything, and why Clearwater's geography raises the stakes.

## Start With the License, Not the Pitch

Florida requires commercial construction work to be performed under a valid state certificate of competency. Under [Florida Statute 489.113](https://www.leg.state.fl.us/Statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0400-0499/0489/Sections/0489.113.html), a certified contractor can legally work in any Florida jurisdiction — no separate local licensing required. You can confirm any contractor's status in minutes through the [Florida DBPR public license lookup](https://www.myfloridalicense.com/wl11.asp): search the name or number, check that the license is active, and look for open disciplinary history.

Florida Construction Specialists holds certified building contractor license **CBC1262722** and has worked in the Tampa Bay region since 1982 — 44 years of continuous commercial construction. Ask any contractor for their license number before the conversation goes further. Hesitation on this point is a reason to keep looking.

## Why Clearwater's Flood Map Changes the Conversation

Clearwater is a different animal from most of FCS's inland service area because it straddles a true barrier island. Clearwater Beach sits on that barrier island, connected to the mainland by causeway, and mainland Clearwater itself runs along the Intracoastal Waterway. Both settings commonly fall inside FEMA Special Flood Hazard Areas. Pinellas County's floodplain rules require new or substantially improved buildings to sit at least one foot above the [Base Flood Elevation](https://pinellas.gov/construction-in-a-floodplain/) — and a commercial GC unfamiliar with that requirement can miss elevation certificates, flood-vent specifications, or the limits on dry floodproofing that a plan reviewer will catch before you break ground.

Layer on the [Florida Building Code's 2023 revision](https://floridabuilding.org/bc/bc_default.aspx), which mandates impact-resistant glazing and stricter fastener schedules in wind-borne debris regions, and a Clearwater commercial project has two separate regulatory systems — flood and wind — that both have to clear plan review at once. A contractor who has only worked inland projects in Hillsborough County may know one of these well and not the other.

## What to Ask Beyond the License

**1. Do you self-perform, or do you subcontract and step back?**
An always-prime GC keeps direct control of the crew, schedule, and quality. A broker signs the contract and hands off responsibility. Ask plainly which one you're hiring.

**2. Do you have in-house engineering and drafting?**
Commercial permits need stamped drawings. A GC with in-house engineering and architectural drafting keeps design intent and field execution aligned, instead of losing time between an outsourced designer and the crew doing the work.

**3. Have you actually built on Clearwater Beach or along the Intracoastal, not just in Pinellas County generally?**
Barrier-island logistics are their own discipline — limited staging space, causeway-controlled deliveries, and tighter parking than an inland commercial lot. A contractor who has only worked mainland sites in Tampa or Brandon may not have planned around any of it.

**4. Can you run multiple trades on one schedule?**
Structural, exterior envelope, and finish work overlapping on a single job needs a GC who coordinates trades directly — not one who juggles separate contracts with separate subs and hopes the schedule holds.

## Clearwater-Specific Considerations

Clearwater's storm exposure compounds its flood-zone rules. Tampa Bay sees two to three tropical systems per season between June and November, and Hurricane Ian in 2022 and Hurricane Idalia in 2023 both drove a wave of commercial restoration work through Pinellas County — some of it handled by contractors with no prior Florida track record who left projects unfinished. On top of storm risk, the region's humid subtropical climate (summer humidity averaging around 75 percent) accelerates deterioration on stucco, concrete, and exterior metal near the water. A GC who has spent decades building in this environment specifies materials and details differently than one who hasn't.
