Bradenton condo boards face two Senate Bill 4-D obligations at once: the milestone structural inspection and the Structural Integrity Reserve Study, or SIRS. The milestone inspection tells you what is physically wrong with the building. The SIRS tells you whether you have the money reserved to fix it. Along the Manatee River, where waterfront towers near the Bradenton Riverwalk have sat in brackish, humid air for decades, the two almost always point at the same repairs.

Neither document is something a contractor signs. A licensed engineer, architect, or reserve specialist produces the [milestone inspection and SIRS](https://condos.myfloridalicense.com/inspections/). What a prime contractor does is the work those studies drive — and price it accurately enough that the reserve numbers hold up.

## The SIRS Is a Repair List With a Price Tag

A Structural Integrity Reserve Study is, in practice, a funded repair plan. It requires the association to reserve money for the components that keep the building standing: roof, load-bearing walls, floor and foundation, fireproofing, plumbing, windows, exterior waterproofing, and any other item costing more than $10,000 to replace. Associations that existed on or before July 1, 2022 had to complete their first SIRS by December 31, 2025, and the old practice of voting to waive reserves is no longer allowed.

That deadline has already passed. Bradenton boards that scrambled to complete a SIRS are now holding a document that names specific structural repairs and, in many cases, a reserve balance that does not cover them. The gap between the two is where the real work starts.

## Pricing the Reserve Items Before They Become Emergencies

The value of bringing in a contractor early is calibration. A reserve study built on generic per-square-foot estimates can be off by a wide margin, especially for structural [concrete restoration](https://floridaconstructionspecialists.com/concrete-restoration/), which varies enormously with how deep the corrosion runs. Florida Construction Specialists has priced and performed these scopes across Tampa Bay since 1982 under Florida license CBC1262722, working as an Always Prime Contractor with an in-house engineer — which means the repair estimate that feeds your reserve plan comes from the crew that would actually do the job, not a spreadsheet.

For a Bradenton waterfront condo, the reserve items most likely to be underfunded are:

- **Exterior waterproofing** — the first line of defense against river humidity, and the one boards most often let lapse.
- **Concrete and balcony restoration** — see [balcony reconstruction](https://floridaconstructionspecialists.com/balcony-reconstruction/), the highest-exposure structural element on any waterfront building.
- **Envelope and window systems** — which have to meet the current Florida Building Code when replaced, not the standard from the original build.

## Bradenton-Specific Considerations

The Manatee River is brackish, not fresh, and that changes the chemistry. Salt content in the air and groundwater near the Riverwalk drives the same chloride corrosion that attacks Gulf-front buildings, just at a slower pace than open coast — enough to catch boards off guard because the damage builds quietly for years before a milestone inspection exposes it. A high water table across much of downtown Bradenton keeps the lowest structural elements damp, which is why waterproofing and concrete repair dominate the reserve conversation here.

Storm exposure sharpens the stakes. With the June-through-November season and recent systems like Hurricane Idalia in 2023 pushing surge up the Manatee, an under-reserved structural repair is a liability that compounds every hurricane season it goes unfunded. Boards that translate their SIRS into a real repair schedule now — rather than treating it as a filed document — are the ones that avoid a special assessment later.
