When a hurricane hits Davis Islands, the damage rarely comes from one direction. The low-lying, man-made islands off Tampa's Bayshore sit in evacuation zone A and a FEMA high-risk flood zone, which means a single storm can bring wind damage to the roof and envelope, surge and flood damage to the ground floor, and water intrusion through both at once. Restoring a property here is as much about navigating two insurance policies as it is about the rebuild itself.

## The Insurance Timeline Moves Faster Than It Used To

Florida's claim rules tightened after the 2022–2024 insurance reforms, and the deadlines now favor homeowners who act quickly. Under [Florida Statute 627.70132](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/ica), you generally have one year from the date of loss to file a new hurricane claim and 18 months for a supplemental claim. Miss the window and the claim is barred, regardless of how real the damage is.

Two other realities shape a Davis Islands claim:

- **Separate hurricane deductibles** run 2 to 5 percent of dwelling coverage, not a flat amount — a meaningful figure on a waterfront home.
- **Flood is a separate policy.** Homeowners insurance excludes rising water, so surge damage is covered only under a flood policy. After a storm, the two claims have to be documented separately even when the damage is tangled together.

## Why Scope Accuracy Decides the Outcome

The most common way a Tampa storm claim goes wrong is an incomplete initial scope. Water that wicks up through a concrete slab, saltwater corrosion in wall assemblies, and hidden structural damage behind intact-looking finishes don't show up on a five-minute adjuster walk-through. If the scope misses them, the settlement misses them too, and the homeowner discovers the gap months later.

FCS documents the full extent of damage — structural, envelope, and water — and coordinates that scope directly with the adjuster. Because the firm carries an in-house engineer and architectural draftsman, structural questions get answered on-site instead of waiting on an outside referral. On surge-damaged Davis Islands ground floors, that often means [concrete restoration and exterior waterproofing](https://floridaconstructionspecialists.com/concrete-restoration/) as part of the rebuild, not just cosmetic repair.

## Tampa-Specific Considerations

Davis Islands concentrates the risk that makes Tampa Bay restoration complex. The islands were built on dredged fill in the 1920s, sit barely above sea level, and face open Hillsborough Bay, so wind and water arrive together. Any rebuild now falls under the Florida Building Code, 8th Edition (2023), which requires flood-resistant construction in Special Flood Hazard Areas and impact-rated glazing in the wind-borne debris region — meaning a storm repair frequently triggers code upgrades the original 1920s–1980s structure never had.

Recent history sets the expectation. Hurricane Ian in 2022 and Idalia in 2023 both pushed surge into low-lying South Tampa, and the region's June-through-November season guarantees the exposure repeats. A homeowner who documents damage immediately, files within the statutory window, and works with a prime contractor who scopes the full repair is the one who avoids an underpaid claim and a half-finished rebuild.
