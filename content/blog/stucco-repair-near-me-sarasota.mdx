Stucco repair in Sarasota starts with figuring out why the stucco cracked or failed in the first place — not just patching over what's visible. A crack, a hollow-sounding section, or a stain near a window is usually a symptom, and repairing the surface without addressing what's happening underneath means the same damage tends to come back within a season or two.

Sarasota sits in the same Tampa Bay climate belt that makes stucco maintenance a recurring reality for property owners across the region: heat, high humidity, and an active hurricane season that puts real stress on exterior finishes year after year.

## What Actually Causes Stucco Damage in Sarasota

Stucco is a rigid, cement-based cladding, which means it doesn't flex the way the wood or metal framing behind it does. As a house settles, as temperatures swing between a hot afternoon and a cooler evening, and as humidity swells and shrinks the materials behind the wall, that rigidity is exactly what produces cracking over time.

A few patterns show up consistently on Sarasota-area homes and commercial buildings:

- **Hairline cracking around windows and doors** — these openings interrupt the wall plane, concentrating stress at the corners
- **Cracking along control joints** — joints that were installed too far apart, or that have failed sealant, let the wall move in ways the stucco can't absorb
- **Staining or discoloration** — often the first visible sign that water is getting behind the finish rather than shedding off it
- **Hollow or "drummy" sections** — a tap test that sounds hollow usually means the stucco has separated from the lath or substrate underneath

Tampa Bay's humid subtropical climate — average summer humidity runs around 75% — keeps moisture in play longer than it would in a drier region. Once water finds a way behind stucco through a cracked joint or a failed flashing detail, it tends to stay there, which is why what looks like a cosmetic crack can be masking wood rot or corroded lath by the time a contractor opens the wall up.

## How a Proper Stucco Repair Works

A repair that's built to last starts with removing all of the damaged and compromised material, not just filling the visible crack. The typical sequence:

1. **Assess the extent of the damage** — sounding the wall for hollow spots, checking for moisture intrusion behind the visible crack, and identifying whether lath or sheathing underneath needs attention
2. **Cut out and remove unsound stucco** back to solid substrate, including any areas where moisture has compromised the material beyond the crack itself
3. **Repair or replace the lath and address any underlying moisture issue** before a single layer of new stucco goes back on
4. **Apply scratch, brown, and finish coats**, matching the original texture pattern so the repair doesn't stand out
5. **Match the color and finish**, either blending into existing paint or scheduling a full recoat when an exact match isn't realistic on a weathered wall

Skipping the substrate and moisture steps is the most common reason a "repaired" stucco crack reappears within a year — the patch looks fine, but the underlying problem that caused it never got fixed.

## What to Do If the Damage Followed a Storm

If stucco cracking or a larger section of damage shows up after a tropical system moves through, treat it as an insurance and documentation question first, repair question second. Photograph the damage as soon as it's safe to do so, and note the date. [Florida Statute 627.70132](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0627/Sections/0627.70132.html) gives homeowners a one-year window from the date of loss to file a hurricane claim, and insurers are far more receptive to claims backed by prompt, dated documentation than damage reported months later.

Whether or not a claim is involved, a licensed contractor should still evaluate the wall before any cosmetic patching happens — storm-driven cracks are exactly the kind that hide moisture intrusion behind them.
